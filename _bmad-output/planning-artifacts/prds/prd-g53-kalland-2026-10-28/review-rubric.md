# PRD Quality Review — CRM-DAM (prd-g53-kalland-2026-10-28)

Calibration: solo, graded course project (IBE160), v1 due 2026-12-05, ~5-page PRD intended, chain-top (feeds architecture → epics/stories). Brevity is not penalised; the bar is that the access rules, which carry the grade and the tests, are complete and that scope follows the teacher's feedback.

## Overall verdict

A tight, concrete PRD. The success criteria are testable, the user journeys are real, the NFRs have numbers, and the out-of-scope list is explicit, so most of it can go straight to stories. Two things are at risk. First, the access model (visibility × price × licence), which the PRD itself treats as the core, leaves gaps: public+free images, previews and archived images, revoking "own" images, and place names in free-text fields. Second, v1 has grown since the brief (cart, ZIP checkout, offline reverse geocoding, action log, side-by-side duplicate review, WCAG 2.2 AA), after the teacher asked for it to shrink, and the PRD gives no priority order or cut line inside v1. Fix the access gaps and add a cut line, and it is ready for architecture.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Most decisions are stated as decisions, not "considerations": "Checkout needs no payment" (FR-26), "v1 has one licence type" (FR-17), "returns 'not found', never 'forbidden'" (FR-16), "the mock is the default provider" (FR-32), "The provider never turns coordinates into place names itself" (FR-9). The `*Later:*` lines after each journey and the Out of Scope list show what was deferred.

What's missing is the cost side of the trade-offs. The PRD never says what v1 gives up by adding a cart and ZIP checkout instead of the brief's simpler "buys a paid one", or by changing the filename-match rule from "accepted with a warning" (brief, Success Criteria 2) to a keep/discard side-by-side review (FR-7). There is no Open Questions section, and only one `[NOTE FOR PM]` (NFR-5). That note is at a real but low-impact checkpoint, not at the real tensions listed below.

### Findings
- **high** No priority order or cut line inside v1 (§ Functional Requirements, § Out of Scope) — The teacher's main feedback was "V1 is large for one person… Choose the core flow." The PRD names the spine in the Overview ("upload → AI metadata → visibility and price → … licence is logged"), but FR-1…FR-35 are all equally mandatory. The only staged item is FR-36. Several FRs go beyond the spine and beyond the brief: cart editing (FR-25), checkout ZIP (FR-26, FR-30), offline reverse geocoding (FR-10), action log with per-user view (FR-35), dashboard "recent activity" (FR-31), and encrypted key override (FR-32). If time runs short there is no agreed order for what to drop. *Fix:* Tag each FR as spine / v1 / cut-first. For example, cart → direct "license this image" button, FR-35 per-user view, FR-31 activity feed, and FR-10 could be cut first. Or add a short "If time runs short" paragraph that names the order.
- **medium** Changes from the brief are not traced to decisions (§ header, FR-7, FR-17, FR-25–26) — The PRD changes several things the brief set: filename duplicates now need a keep/discard choice, price is in USD (the brief's Success Criteria 5 said "0 kr"), cart + checkout + ZIP are new, and SC-5's "invoicing view" is now "paid licences only". `docs/process/decisions.md` stops at D-021, and none of its entries covers these changes. Process counts 30 %, and CLAUDE.md requires a D-xxx when the plan changes. *Fix:* Log a D-022 (or more) covering the PRD's changes from the brief, and cite it in the PRD header next to the brief link.

## Substance over theater — strong

No persona theater: there are two named protagonists (Ida, Jonas), each driving concrete FRs. There's no Vision section to inflate, and no differentiation section (correct for a PRD that builds on the brief). NFRs have product-specific thresholds: "about 500 images and 10 client organisations… under 1 s" (NFR-4), "at most 250 characters (aim for under 150)" (NFR-6), and the exact seed set (NFR-1). The addendum keeps the depth (metadata field mapping, pilot numbers, upload-size reasoning) out of the main body, which is the right split.

### Findings
- **low** "works smoothly" (§ NFR-4) — This adjective is filler next to real thresholds. *Fix:* Delete "smoothly"; the numbers carry it.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is clear and borrowed honestly from the teacher: the minimal end-to-end spine, plus the access matrix as the thing that must be right. Success Criteria 1–3 test exactly that thesis. Counter-metrics don't apply at this stake level.

Where coherence weakens: Success Criteria 9 is titled "Search finds what staff look for", but in mock mode it tests the search engine against fixture metadata, not whether staff find images. Also, the scope has grown beyond the spine (see Decision-readiness), so parts of the FR list read like a backlog.

### Findings
- **medium** Success Criteria 9 depends on fixture metadata that is never specified (§ Success Criteria 9, FR-9, NFR-1) — The brief describes the mock as returning "fixed, predictable metadata". If it returns the same metadata for every image, a search test per keyword category proves nothing. Nothing says the sample images ship with their own per-image mock metadata. *Fix:* State in NFR-1 or FR-32 that the mock returns per-image fixture metadata for the sample set, covering all seven keyword categories, and rename Success Criteria 9 to something like "Search returns expected sample images per keyword category".

## Done-ness clarity — thin

This is the dimension the access tests and stories will lean on most, and it has the most gaps. Many FRs are well bounded (FR-2 "expires after 7 days", FR-5 "JPEG and PNG up to 50 MB", FR-11 "at most 15 keywords", FR-16 "not found, never forbidden", FR-29 "no embedded thumbnail or preview"). But the matrix that Success Criteria 1 promises to test exhaustively is not fully defined by the FRs, and two privacy rules contradict other requirements.

### Findings
- **high** The access matrix has undefined cells (§ FR-16, FR-18, FR-24, FR-27; Success Criteria 1) — Success Criteria 1 says tests cover "every combination of visibility × price × licence held", but the FRs only define "restricted + free = own" (FR-18). Unspecified: (a) **public + free**: can a client download it directly, and does that record a 0 USD licence, or does it go through cart/checkout? FR-24 says download is offered only "if the organisation has the image", which suggests the client must check out at 0 USD. (b) **restricted to several clients + free**: is it "own" for every listed client? FR-18 says "restricted to a client". (c) **own but not yet downloaded**: FR-27 blocks adding to the cart only for images "the organisation already holds a licence to", so an own image could be "bought". (d) **internal-only + paid with no licence**: presumably not found, but only by inference. An engineer would have to invent these answers, and the tests would encode the guesses. *Fix:* Add a compact truth table (visibility × price × licence/own → visible / preview / download / buy) to the PRD or addendum, and point FR-16, FR-18, FR-24 and FR-27 at it.
- **high** Client copies must have "no place names", but place names are part of the metadata (§ FR-9, FR-10, FR-28, FR-29; Success Criteria 6) — FR-9 generates "place" keywords, and the AI gets a GPS-derived place name, so titles and descriptions will naturally say things like "Bergen harbour in winter". FR-28 says every download carries "the current, edited metadata", and FR-29 says a client copy has "no place names in EXIF, XMP or IPTC". Both can't hold for free-text fields. The addendum's "rebuilt from a whitelist" handles structured location fields (city, country, sublocation) but not prose. As written, Success Criteria 6 either fails on most AI-tagged images or needs the metadata to be silently altered. *Fix:* Decide and state one of these: (1) "no place names" means only the structured location fields and the derived place-name record, and staff are responsible for prose; (2) place-category keywords are dropped from client copies, and title/description are left alone; or (3) the AI prompt avoids place names in title/description/alt-text. Then make the checklist match. Log it as a decision, since it refines D-011.
- **medium** Previews and on-screen images aren't covered by the location rule (§ FR-23, FR-24, FR-29) — FR-29 applies to downloads. The client-facing preview (FR-23) and the larger image on the image page (FR-24) are also files sent to the client's browser, and could carry EXIF GPS if they are made by resizing the original. *Fix:* Extend FR-29 to "every image file served to a client (preview, image page, download, ZIP)".
- **medium** Revoking an "own" image doesn't hold (§ FR-18, FR-19) — If staff revoke the 0 USD licence on an image that is still restricted to the client and free, FR-18 makes it the client's own again right away, and the next download records a new licence. FR-19's revoke only takes effect on paid or no-longer-own images. *Fix:* State that revoking also requires (or prompts for) removing the client from the visibility list, or that a revoked licence blocks FR-18 for that client–image pair.
- **medium** Archived images and direct access (§ FR-16, FR-20) — FR-20 hides archived images "from search and browsing". FR-16, the visibility rule, doesn't mention archiving, so a restricted, listed client with no licence could still open an archived image by URL, and it could still count as "own" under FR-18. *Fix:* Add "not archived" to FR-16's visibility conditions for clients without a licence, and to FR-18.
- **medium** Shared drafts and auto-save have no conflict rule (§ FR-13, FR-14) — "All staff can see and edit drafts" and "Edits are saved automatically", but nothing says what happens when two staff edit the same field at once, or when auto-save fails. *Fix:* One line, e.g. "last write wins per field; a failed save shows an error and keeps the unsaved value".
- **low** Undefined dashboard content (§ FR-31, UJ-3 step 2) — "recent activity" and "new images the agency has made available" have no definition: activity by whom, how many items, new since when? *Fix:* For example, "the last 10 licences and downloads by any contact of the organisation; images made visible to it in the last 30 days or since the contact's last login".
- **low** Duplicates within a batch and filename matching (§ FR-7) — It doesn't cover two identical files in the same batch, or whether filename matching ignores case or extension. *Fix:* Add "within the batch as well as against the library; filename match ignores case".
- **low** Accessibility has no check method (§ NFR-5) — "aims for WCAG 2.2 AA" has no verification step. *Fix:* Name the check, e.g. "automated axe check with zero serious violations on the main pages plus a manual keyboard pass".
- **low** "consistent capitalisation" (§ FR-11) — Not testable as written. *Fix:* Name the rule (e.g. "keywords lower-case except proper nouns").

## Scope honesty — adequate

The Out of Scope list is explicit and long. Each journey closes with what comes later, and FR-36 is clearly marked as stretch. NFR-9 says plainly that the data model allows more without building it. There are no `[ASSUMPTION]` tags. A few inferences deserve one, for example that the offline reverse geocoder covers the sample images' locations (FR-10), and that "about 10 s" AI time applies only to the real provider (NFR-4). The open-items density (one NOTE, no OQ section) is low, which is fine at these stakes, but it hides the real open decisions in Done-ness above.

### Findings
- **medium** NFR-9 brings back the criterion the teacher called untestable (§ NFR-9) — "allows, without a rewrite" is the wording the teacher's feedback flagged ("can be extended later without a rewrite… cannot be tested"). The list after it is concrete, so the fix is wording. *Fix:* Rephrase as checkable schema facts: "licence types are a table with price and currency; every price column has a currency code; images and file versions are separate tables; providers implement one interface; top-level tables have `tenant_id`".
- **low** A contact leaving has no FR (§ FR-19, FR-1) — FR-19 says licences survive "a contact leaving", but no FR lets staff deactivate a single contact. Only the organisation has a status (FR-1, FR-3). *Fix:* Add contact deactivation to FR-1/FR-3, or list it in Out of Scope.

## Downstream usability — adequate

IDs are contiguous and unique (UJ-1…4, FR-1…36, NFR-1…9), the FRs are grouped F1–F9, and the one in-text cross-reference (FR-11 → NFR-6) resolves. Staff and client are defined at the top of the FR section, which acts as a mini-glossary. Gaps for story creation: there's no Glossary, the core access nouns drift (below), FRs don't point back to journeys or success criteria, and UJ-4 is not standalone.

### Findings
- **medium** Core access terms drift (§ Success Criteria 4–5, UJ-2, UJ-3, FR-18, FR-22) — The same concept is called "included" (Success Criteria 4–5, UJ-2 step 2, UJ-3 step 3), "the client's own" (Success Criteria 1, FR-18), "free" (FR-15), and "own under FR-18" (FR-22). "Client", "client organisation" and "organisation" are used interchangeably, and so are "staff" and "admin" (UJ-4, NFR-1 "one staff user", brief "admin"). These are exactly the nouns the access tests are named after. *Fix:* Add a five-line glossary (Staff, Client/Organisation, Contact, Own image, Licence) and use "own" consistently.
- **medium** UJ-4 is not a journey and isn't self-contained (§ UJ-4) — It has no protagonist and no steps, and it defers to another document: "The brief covers these tasks". Story creation pulling UJ-4 alone gets a pointer. *Fix:* Either turn it into a short journey (e.g. "Ida onboards a new client and revokes a licence", 4–5 steps that touch FR-1/2, FR-19, FR-32–34), or rename it "Admin capabilities" and list the FR IDs.
- **low** No FR ↔ success criteria/journey trace (§ Success Criteria, FRs) — For a project graded on plan → story → commit traceability, a "Covers: SC-1" tag on each FR, or a trace line under each success criterion, would make story acceptance criteria mechanical. The success criteria also have no IDs (just 1–9). *Fix:* Number them SC-1…SC-9 and add a short trace.

## Shape fit — strong

A two-sided product (staff, clients) with real UX gets user journeys with named protagonists, and that's what is here, at about the right density: two strong staff journeys, one client journey and a stub. There's no regulatory section beyond what's needed (NFR-5 on public-sector accessibility, NFR-3 on privacy). The addendum holds the architecture-level detail, which suits a chain-top PRD. UJ-4's shape is the one weak spot (above).

- **Cross-references:** FR-11 → NFR-6 resolves. The relative links to the brief, the brief's addendum, `research/technical-metadata-libraries-2026-10-28/research.md` and `research/pilot-haiku-metadata-2026-10-28/results.md` were not checked for existence beyond the brief folder, which exists. The addendum cites "section C" of the metadata research; check that the anchor exists.
- **Success criteria vs FRs:** Success Criteria 8 lists "upload, edit, publish and revoke", while FR-35 also lists visibility/price change and archive. FR-35 is the superset; align Success Criteria 8 or say "at least".
- **Currency:** USD is used throughout the PRD; the brief's Success Criteria 5 used "kr". Fine if a decision records it (see Decision-readiness).
- **Assumptions Index:** No `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and no index, so nothing to roundtrip.
- **Front matter:** The title says "g53-kalland — CRM/DAM" and the H1 says "CRM-DAM". This is minor; pick one product name.
- **Required sections:** Present for these stakes: overview, success criteria, journeys, FRs, NFRs, out of scope. An Open Questions section is missing; add one holding the high and medium items above until they're decided.
