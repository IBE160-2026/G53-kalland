#### IIM truncation: my suggestion
**The problem.** IPTC-IIM, the older format some tools still read, limits fields in *bytes*: title 64, description 2,000, each keyword 64. In UTF-8, æ, ø and å take 2 bytes each, so a title of only æøå letters fits just 32 characters. If you cut at byte 64, you can split a letter in half and write invalid text. XMP has no such limit.

| Option | What it means | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. XMP only, no IIM** | No truncation at all | Older tools that read only IIM show nothing; FR-34 and the research's metadata contract change |
| **B. Write IIM, shortened safely** ← my pick | The app shortens title and description at a whole word, and never mid-letter. A keyword too long for IIM is left out of IIM only. IIM is declared UTF-8 (`CodedCharacterSet`). XMP and EXIF always hold the full value | One small function plus a test with æøå right at the limit; about 1 h |
| **C. Cap the fields themselves** | Title and keywords limited to 64 bytes when entered | Byte limits confuse users ("32 or 64 characters, depending on the letters"), and AI output has to obey them |

**Why B:** it keeps FR-34's promise and costs little, and no reader ever gets broken text. A would be its fallback if the time runs out. In the PRD, FR-34 would get one sentence: "IIM values over the byte limit are shortened at a whole character; XMP keeps the full value." The details go in the addendum. Do you agree?
